What I do

Routine and specialized foot care for adults of all ages, with particular attention to clients who find it difficult to reach or care for their own feet.

Routine nail care

Trimming, filing and shaping of thickened or hard-to-reach toenails.

Callus & corn care

Careful reduction of hardened skin and pressure points, so walking feels easier.

Ingrown nail care

Relief and ongoing management of nails that curve into the skin.

Thickened & fungal nails

Reduction and tidying of thick or discoloured nails, with advice on care between visits.

Diabetic foot care

Certified diabetic foot assessment with routine checks of circulation, sensation and skin integrity.

Wound care

As a Certified Wound Care Nurse, I manage acute, chronic and slow-healing wounds at home.

Education & prevention

Practical guidance on footwear, moisturizing, and which changes are worth a call.

A different kind of appointment

Feet don't exist in isolation. Neither do the people they belong to.

Alongside my nursing career, I spent a year and a half studying Ayurveda — at the Ayurvedic Institute here in the United States and in Tamil Nadu, India — and years teaching yoga. That training shaped how I practice. I look at circulation, posture, gait, footwear and daily habits, not just the nails in front of me. Often what's causing discomfort isn't where the discomfort is.

It also means I don't rush. Appointments are unhurried on purpose, because the conversation is part of the care. Many of my clients have been with me for years, and I like it that way.

About Lynn

I've been a nurse for more than twenty years, most of that time here in Albuquerque, and a good deal of it spent on home-based foot and wound care. I've looked after thousands of people over the course of my career. I started Global Footcare in 2019 to do that work the way I always believed it should be done: in someone's own home, without a clock running, with enough time to actually listen.

I hold board certification as both a Certified Foot Care Nurse and a Certified Wound Care Nurse, and I've worked as a diabetes educator. That combination matters more than it sounds: the feet that need the most careful attention usually belong to someone managing diabetes, circulation trouble, or a wound that won't close.

I also tend to stay in my clients' lives. I remember birthdays, I call to see how people are getting on, and I notice when something seems different. For a lot of the people I see, that attention matters as much as the care itself.

Once a month I provide volunteer foot care at the Albuquerque Westside Homeless Shelter. Feet carry people through hard circumstances, and everyone deserves to have theirs looked after.
